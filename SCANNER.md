# Independent CoinDCX market scanner

This service adds a USDT futures scanner without changing the Telegram gold bot. It uses its own process, Docker Compose project, SQLite database and health port. It excludes XAU, never adopts pre-existing positions, and reuses the existing `.env` CoinDCX and Telegram credentials. There is no Google Sheets dependency.

| Entry | Conditions | Margin and leverage | Margin mode | Take profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short | More than 35% gain in 24 hours; price within 10% below the nearest confirmed weekly resistance; resting sell limit 2% above that resistance | $3 margin, 3×, sized at the limit price | Crossed | Close 75% (or the minimum valid quantity) at a 7% price decline; remaining quantity at 20%, from the actual average entry |
| Long | Executable market price at or below 110% of the available-history all-time low, with completed 4h consolidation or bullish reversal | $6 margin, 1× | Isolated | 6% price increase from actual fill |

No stop-loss is submitted by this scanner. Gold retains its existing settings. The initial account-wide non-gold limit is five positions: three shorts and two longs. The first successful total-USDT-wallet read is saved as the permanent baseline. Every full $50 increase above that baseline adds one slot, alternating short then long: +$50 gives four shorts/two longs, +$100 gives four shorts/three longs, and +$150 gives five shorts/three longs. Slot growth uses `total_wallet_balance` from the signed cross-margin-details endpoint, not free balance or unrealized PnL. Opening a trade therefore does not shrink the cap. A later balance decline can reduce the cap, never below the original five; existing positions are not forcibly closed. Failed balance reads block new entries. The stored baseline survives restarts and is shared by discovery and execution. This includes external positions (including DOGE), pending exchange orders, and durable scanner submissions whose outcome is uncertain. Gold is excluded. Positions and reservations for the same pair are counted once. Pending opposite-side quantities covered by an existing position count as closing orders rather than additional long/short slots; quantities exceeding that position still reserve the reversal side. The scanner checks account capacity before discovery/candle analysis and again before entry submission. If full, over either side limit, or the account read fails, no new entries are submitted. One scanner position per coin; externally owned positions are counted but never modified. Existing positions above the new limits remain open and block further entries until capacity becomes available. A coin with a realized profit transaction is blocked from a fresh initial entry until the next midnight in Asia/Kolkata, with this lock stored across restarts. The explicitly rearmed averaging allowance after a confirmed first profit exit is permitted within that same still-open position despite this profit lock. Unknown exit PnL blocks that coin until reconciliation succeeds.

Both strategies require at least 100 days of available CoinDCX futures history. The scanner fetches up to 1,000 completed daily candles for weekly resistance, groups them into complete Monday–Sunday UTC weeks, and uses swing highs with two strictly lower completed weeks on each side. It selects the nearest resistance strictly above price. Incomplete weeks and gaps cannot confirm resistance. When price is between 90% and 100% of that resistance (excluding the resistance itself), an eligible short uses a resting `limit_order` at **resistance × 1.02**, rounded upward to a valid exchange tick. For example, resistance at 100 places the short limit at 102. The 10% eligibility band is still measured from the original resistance. Current last/mark and bid are checked before submission so the order is intended to rest above market. Exchange price bounds are also checked; an invalid level is skipped, never replaced by a market entry.

An unfilled short limit reserves a short slot and remains at its original price for up to four hours. CoinDCX accepts it as `good_till_cancel`; the running bot enforces the deadline, persists it across restarts, and cancels overdue orders on recovery. The slot remains reserved until the exchange confirms cancellation. A fresh scan may reassess and submit another eligible order after confirmed cancellation. A partial fill triggers cancellation of the remaining entry quantity, then the bot manages the filled amount. Cancellation retries are bounded, with uncertainty retaining the slot and generating an alert. If external activity pushes account capacity over the limits, pending scanner limits are cancelled.

Short exits use two stages. After the entry fill is confirmed, the scanner places a **resting GTC BUY limit at 7% below the actual average entry**, closing 75% of the short quantity rounded down to the exchange lot increment. If that split is too small, it uses the minimum valid partial quantity rounded up. The remainder targets a 20% decline. For example, a 3 ORCA short at 2.958 plans a 2.2 ORCA limit at 2.750, leaving 0.8 ORCA for 2.366. A 0.5 NMR short at 16.767 with a 0.1 lot step and $6 minimum instead plans 0.4 at 15.593, leaving 0.1 for 13.413. These are price changes, before fees and funding.

The 7% order rests at the exchange before its price is reached; it can fill during a wick between bot checks. It has no four-hour expiry or market-order timeout. A touch does not guarantee a full fill: price, available liquidity and order priority determine execution. Partial fills remain on the exchange for the unfilled quantity. The bot confirms the entire planned first exit before rearming averaging.

The existing full-position native TP is cancelled and cancellation verified **before** placing the sized limit. The native 20% TP is attached **after** that limit fills, on the next reconciliation. This avoids two independent BUY exits competing for the entire short. The final native TP may close a remainder below the minimum notional for a separate order. If the final target has already been reached when the bot reconciles, it requests a full-position market close for the remainder. A pre-upgrade market exit already submitted is reconciled to completion without a duplicate limit.

Each limit intent and exchange order ID are stored durably before any replacement. The bot cancels its own old limit and confirms cancellation before repricing it after an attributed manual or automatic addition. It also cancels its limit when it observes closure, reversal, an unexpected reduction, changed position settings, or another pending BUY. Other/manual orders are preserved and block a new profit limit. The bot never treats an unconfirmed cancellation or request timeout as permission to send another order. Rejected, externally cancelled or incomplete cancelled exits retain a native final TP where possible and require review, without rearming averaging.

The public API does not document a reduce-only flag for these sized limit orders. They are ordinary opposite-side orders checked against the live position, not a guarantee against reversal during concurrent manual activity or an outage. Keep the bot running to reconcile changes; cancel its resting close limit before manually closing or reversing the position while the bot is stopped. Stopping the bot does not cancel exchange orders. While the first limit can fill offline, attachment of the remaining 20% TP requires the bot to run.

New initial shorts are skipped if a minimum-size partial exit cannot leave a valid positive remainder or other exchange limits fail; the $3 initial margin budget is not increased. An existing short that cannot leave a valid remainder retains its full-position 7% TP and alerts. Limit price ticks, minimum notional, fresh position size, competing orders and exchange LTP price bands are checked before submission. If the 7% limit is currently outside the permitted price band, the bot keeps the native 20% TP, alerts and rechecks after a minute. It does not send repeated invalid orders or silently move the target. Once eligible, it confirms removal of the native TP and places the first limit. The scanner normally reconciles about every five seconds plus API latency; API uncertainty can require manual review.

A filled scanner short initially has **one averaging allowance**, stored in SQLite across restarts. When the executable bid is strictly more than 35% above its first actual fill, the bot can place one further sell limit at the nearest confirmed weekly resistance × 1.02. The same 10% proximity band, completed daily/weekly history rules and four-hour expiry apply. The initial averaging order uses the exact quantity filled by the first entry, at the same 3× leverage and crossed mode. Equal quantity at a higher price needs more margin than the original $3. The 35% 24-hour pump requirement applies only to the initial entry. Combined position size must fit the exchange leverage tier. Adding to an existing pair uses its existing slot, but an account above its current total/side caps blocks the addition.

Only after the planned first profit limit is fully filled and confirmed does the bot rearm **one averaging attempt at $3 margin and 3×**, rounded down to a valid quantity. Its +35% threshold is measured from the remaining position's average entry. On a subsequent averaging fill, the whole remaining short gets fresh staged targets (with the same minimum-size fallback) from its new weighted-average entry. After that cycle's own first profit exit completes, one more $3 attempt may be rearmed. Order and fill history are retained, including the cost of the remaining quantity after reductions, so old additions are not counted again and realized partial exits cannot masquerade as new averaging entries.

The bot reads same-side pending orders, fills and the position before adding. A manual pending addition, a manual fill in the current cycle, or an unexplained quantity increase consumes the current averaging allowance. It cancels only its own outstanding averaging order when manual activity is detected. Filled manual entries must link to the same position in the transaction ledger and explain quantity and average entry before TP changes. A fully confirmed first profit exit snapshots already-accounted manual fills; these older additions do not consume the newly rearmed allowance, but any new manual addition or still-pending manual order does. Unattributed reductions, reversals, position-ID changes or unexplained balances require review.

A claimed allowance remains consumed when its averaging attempt is rejected, expires or has an ambiguous acknowledgement; observing a subsequently cancelled manual pending addition also consumes it. Only the next confirmed first profit exit or a separate new position resets the allowance. Averaging partial fills cancel the remaining order and use the actual weighted average. An outstanding averaging order is cancelled when the base closes, with the reservation retained until cancellation is confirmed. Exchange-native TP and an averaging order are independent: simultaneous manual activity/fills can race between API reads. Unexpected balances are blocked for review without a replacement order. Do not delete the state database while positions remain open.

Long eligibility uses the complete available CoinDCX futures history, paginated back to before the exchange existed and cached in SQLite. This is the futures contract's available-history low, not a global spot-market ATL. Missing daily candles block eligibility. The current 24-hour low is included so a fresh low is not ignored. The cache advances as new daily candles complete.

ATL proximity alone never opens a long. Six completed four-hour candles must either consolidate inside a 4% high/low range with no fresh low in the final two bars, or the latest bar must be bullish, close above the previous three highs, and hold a higher low than the previous bar. Current price must still hold the confirmation lows. Four-hour bars are assembled from complete sets of documented one-hour API candles; missing, stale and forming bars cannot confirm entry. Confirmation is rechecked before submitting. Existing scanner longs are reconciled to the new 6% TP; there is no forced portfolio rebalance or automatic loss exit.

Quantities normally round down. Initial short quantity and estimated $3 margin are computed from the limit price 2% above resistance, not the lower current market price. If a $6 long is below the exchange's minimum, it rounds up only to the smallest valid quantity, provided its estimated margin is no more than $6.50. Larger exchange minimums are skipped. Minimum notional is checked using the lower of the executable, last and fresh mark prices; the margin cap uses the higher price. Sizing is refreshed after preparation, immediately before submission. This avoids sizing solely from an ask price that is above the exchange mark/reference price. Short sizing stays within $3 estimated margin. Market fills can differ from the sizing quote; fees and funding are separate. TP prices round outward to a valid tick and are verified against the filled position.

Order intent is committed before submission. A timeout does not trigger another entry order. Uncertain orders retain a slot and generate an alert for review. Definite exchange rejections retain a sanitized error message and apply a durable 15-minute per-coin cooldown instead of resubmitting on every scan. Market prices can still move before exchange validation, so no implementation can promise zero future API rejections. Position quantity, direction, margin mode and leverage are checked before subsequent position mutations. Manual same-side additions to tracked shorts are reconciled as described above; unexplained position changes remain blocked for review. Do not delete the state database while positions remain open.

## Deploy on the VPS

Keep the gold service running. These commands only operate on the separate scanner project. If the VPS checkout contains unrelated local changes, preserve them before updating Git; do not reset or overwrite the gold code.

```sh
cd /home/ubuntu/growonspot
# Update the checkout once any existing local changes have been preserved.
git pull --ff-only
# Create only on first setup; retain existing scanner settings on later deploys.
test -f .env.scanner || cp .env.scanner.example .env.scanner
chmod 600 .env.scanner
mkdir -p scanner-data
docker compose -p growonspot-scanner -f docker-compose.scanner.yml build scanner
# Authenticated reads only: no orders and no Telegram test messages.
docker compose -p growonspot-scanner -f docker-compose.scanner.yml run --rm scanner python -m scanner --check
# Enables live scanning with SCANNER_ENABLED=true from .env.scanner.
docker compose -p growonspot-scanner -f docker-compose.scanner.yml up -d scanner
curl -fsS http://127.0.0.1:8081/health
docker compose -p growonspot-scanner -f docker-compose.scanner.yml logs --tail=100 -f scanner
```

Discovery is configured for every five minutes (`SCANNER_SCAN_SECONDS=300`). A resting limit can execute at the exchange between scans. `SCANNER_SHORT_DISTANCE=0.10` sets the maximum distance below resistance, and `SCANNER_SHORT_LIMIT_SECONDS=14400` sets the four-hour cancellation deadline.

The first full scan downloads and caches history; it can take several minutes. Subsequent scans reuse completed daily history. Health reports discovery count, scan progress, completion timestamp and occupied scanner slots plus total account longs/shorts. A capacity pause is a healthy idle state, not a process failure. An uncertain exchange outcome makes health return `review_required`; a read failure returns `degraded`. Docker health status does not itself restart a container. The restart policy recovers exited processes, while SQLite and the process lock prevent duplicate active instances sharing the same state.

Set `SCANNER_ENABLED=false` in `.env.scanner` and recreate **only this service** to pause entries while retaining reconciliation and TP management. Stopping the service stops monitoring and alerts; exchange TP orders already accepted remain at the exchange. Resting entry limits also remain at the exchange until cancelled, and can fill while the bot is stopped. To stop trading entirely, cancel pending scanner entry orders as well; do not assume that stopping Docker cancels orders.

Telegram alerts use `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` and `TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID`: resting profit limits, their cancellations, confirmed partial profit exits and rearming, split-TP limitations, manual averaging detection, averaging submission/fills/cancellation/errors, resting short limits, cancellation requests/confirmation, confirmed fills, order errors/uncertainty, TP attachment issues and confirmed exits. Failed alert delivery is logged separately. `.env`, `.env.scanner` and `scanner-data/` are excluded from Git and the Docker build context.

## Verify locally without orders

```sh
.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s tests -q
.venv/bin/python -m scanner --inspect
```

The automated suite uses mocked exchange writes. `--inspect` uses public data only; `--check` also validates signed read endpoints. Neither submits trades. API integration follows the [CoinDCX official API reference](https://docs.coindcx.com/).
